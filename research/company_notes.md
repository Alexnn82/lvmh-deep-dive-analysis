# LVMH Company Notes
Although LVMH operates across multiple luxury categories, the company's profitability is heavily concentrated in Fashion & Leather Goods. As a result, the long-term health of flagship brands such as Louis Vuitton and Dior is likely the single most important driver of future cash flow and valuation. 
## Business Overview
LVMH operates through five primary business segments: 
Fashion & Leather Goods
Selective Retailing
Watches & Jewelry
Perfumes & Cosmetics
Wines & Spirits
LVMH generated 80.8 billion of revenue and 17.8 billion of profit from recurring operations in 2025, corresponding to an operating margin of approximately 22%. This indicates substantial pricing power and operating efficiency across the company's brand.
For every 100E spent, 22E becomes operating profit and 13.5E becomes shareholder profit. The gap between net margin and operating margin is not very large which is a good sign, a large gap could signal heavy taxes or debt.
LVMH generated 10.9 billion in net profit attributable to shareholders, representing a net margin of approximately 13.5%. The company's ability to convert revenue into bottom-line earnings reflects strong operating profitability and disciplined financial management.
LVMH generated 11.3 billion of free cash flow in 2025, slightly exceeding reported net profit. This suggests earnings quality is strong and reported profits are being converted into real cash generation. LVMH can generate large amounts of cash because customers are willing to pay prices far above the production cost of its products due to the perceived brand status.
Leather goods has a high revenue margin, this means the theory that people overpay what the raw material cost is because of the brand. LVMH's economics are driven disproportionately by its fashion and leather goods segment, which generates roughly half of revenue but nearly three-quarters of operating profit through pricing power and brand strength. 
## Revenue Drivers
Fashion & Leather goods is the largest segment, representing roughly 47% of revenue and serving as the primary driver of company sales. Selective retailing contributes 23% of revenue through Sephora and DFS. Watches and 
jewelry and perfumes and cosmetics provide diversification and additonal growth avenues. 
## Profit Drivers
Fashion & Leather Goods generates roughly 72% of segment operating profit and is the primary value driver of the company. Wine & Spirits produces strong margins despite representing a relatively small share of revenue. Selective retailing generates substantial revenue but lower margins than the core luxury segments. 
## Competitive Advantages
Brand Heritage and Prestige: LVMH brands have been considered a luxury status symbol for decades. 
Pricing Power: Customers will continue to buy even when prices are raised due to brand value
Quality Reputation and Craftsmanship: Customers believe LVMH products represent superior quality.
## Risks
Revenue has declined from 86.1B Euros in 2023 to 80.8B euros in 2025, raising concerns that luxury demand weakness may be more persistent than expected. 
LVMH is highly dependent on its Fashion & Leather Goods segment, which generates approximately 47% of revenue and 72% of operating profit. Any deterioration in the performance of key brands such as Louis Vuitton or Dior could have a disproportionate impact on profitability and cash flow. 
LVMH's pricing power depends on perceived exclusivity. The widespread availability of counterfeit luxury products could reduce the exclusivity associated with LVMH's brands. 
## Geographic Insight
LVMH maintains a globally diversified revenue base, with Asia ex Japan, The United States, and Europe each contributing approximately 25% of revenue. This geographic diversification reduces dependence on any single consumer market. 
## Initial Investment Thesis
LVMH appears to be a high-quality business with strong brand equity, substantial pricing power, and significant free cash flow generation. Despite recent revenue declines, the company generated 11.3 billion in free cash flow during 2025 and maintained strong profitability, particularly within its fashion and leather goods segment. The investment case largely depends on whether recent revenue weakness reflects a temporary slowdown in luxury demand or a mroe permanent deterioration in consumer interest.
Further analysis is required to determine whether the current market valuation appropriately reflects LVMH's long-term cash flow potential and brand durability.
